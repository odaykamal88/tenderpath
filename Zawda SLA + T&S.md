\# 2\) Terms of Service — detailed review

\#\# A. The service description should be sharper  
Right now, the terms say Zawda provides:

\- regulatory intelligence  
\- compliance monitoring  
\- licensing facilitation  
\- risk advisory and screening tools  
\- alerts and roadmap guidance

That is fine, but if your audience is \*\*CFOs and compliance officers at VASPs\*\*, I recommend defining the service more precisely.

\#\#\# Why this matters  
If Zawda is helping with:  
\- licensing workflows  
\- document management  
\- compliance calendars  
\- regulatory tracking  
\- internal audit preparation

then the contract should make clear Zawda is providing \*\*workflow and intelligence support\*\*, not acting as:  
\- the legal representative  
\- the compliance officer of record  
\- the auditor  
\- the regulated entity’s outsourced governance function

\#\#\# What to do  
Add a clause like:

\> Zawda provides regulatory intelligence, operational compliance support, and licensing workflow facilitation for internal business use by regulated and prospective virtual asset service providers. Zawda does not replace the User’s internal compliance function, legal counsel, audit function, or regulatory filings responsibility.

\---

\#\# B. The “not legal advice” clause is good, but should be more detailed  
You already say Zawda is not a law firm and nothing is legal advice. Good.

\#\#\# What’s missing  
For a VASP audience, the contract should also say:  
\- users remain responsible for \*\*independent legal review\*\*  
\- Zawda’s templates, assessments, and summaries are \*\*not legal opinions\*\*  
\- no one should rely on the platform as a substitute for \*\*local counsel in each jurisdiction\*\*

\#\#\# Suggested addition  
\> Any reports, templates, summaries, or roadmap guidance are general informational tools only and do not constitute legal advice, legal opinion, regulatory clearance, or a substitute for jurisdiction-specific advice from qualified counsel.

\---

\#\# C. “Licensing facilitation” needs careful wording  
This phrase can be risky if it sounds like Zawda is actually helping obtain a license in a regulated sense.

\#\#\# Why it matters  
If Zawda appears to be “facilitating” licensing too strongly, a regulator or client could argue Zawda is making promises or acting beyond a support role.

\#\#\# Better wording  
Use:  
\- “licensing support”  
\- “licensing roadmap assistance”  
\- “document preparation support”  
\- “compliance process facilitation”

Instead of overly broad wording like “facilitation” without limits.

\---

\#\# D. No guarantee clause should be expanded  
You already disclaim outcomes, which is excellent. I would make it even clearer that Zawda does not guarantee:

\- regulator acceptance of any filing  
\- successful licensing approval  
\- avoidance of enforcement action  
\- sufficiency of a compliance program  
\- audit approval  
\- classification of an activity as compliant in every jurisdiction

\#\#\# Why this matters for VASPs  
VASPs often want certainty. The contract should make it impossible to argue that Zawda guaranteed a result.

\---

\#\# E. Acceptable use should mention regulated use more clearly  
The current acceptable use clause is solid, but for this business, I would add:  
\- no use to evade AML/CFT obligations  
\- no use to submit false or misleading information to regulators  
\- no use for sanctions evasion, fraud, or unlawful structuring

\#\#\# Why  
Because your user base is compliance-sensitive, this helps protect the platform and aligns with AML/CFT expectations.

\---

\#\# F. Data protection needs a more practical clause  
The privacy section is acceptable, but it would benefit from more detail around:  
\- customer data roles  
\- controller/processor status  
\- cross-border transfers  
\- retention and deletion process  
\- sub-processors  
\- data subject requests

\#\#\# Why this matters  
VASPs and CFOs will care about:  
\- where documents are stored  
\- whether data leaves the UAE  
\- whether sensitive compliance docs are encrypted  
\- whether audit logs exist

\#\#\# Suggested direction  
Add a clause stating:  
\- the user remains the controller or data owner where applicable  
\- Zawda acts as processor/service provider for uploaded content  
\- Zawda may use subprocessors to operate the service  
\- cross-border transfers comply with applicable law and policy

\---

\#\# G. Liability cap is useful but may need commercial adjustment  
The liability cap says total liability is limited to fees paid in the prior 12 months.

That is common, but for enterprise compliance clients, you may want:  
\- a higher cap for enterprise plans  
\- separate carve-outs for confidentiality breach, willful misconduct, fraud, or data protection violations  
\- a specific cap per contract or per claim

\#\#\# Why  
If a client is paying substantial annual fees, they may negotiate this.

\---

\#\# H. Indemnity clause may be too broad for enterprise clients  
The indemnity is one-sided and broad, which is usually good for the platform, but some clients may push back.

\#\#\# Consider whether to add clarity around:  
\- indemnity for unlawful use  
\- inaccurate user-uploaded information  
\- breach of laws by the client  
\- misuse of deliverables

That will make it stronger and more enforceable.

\---

\#\# I. Governing law and jurisdiction are fine  
DIFC law and DIFC Courts is a reasonable choice if Zawda operates from that ecosystem.

Just make sure the business actually wants:  
\- DIFC law  
\- DIFC Courts  
\- no arbitration

If you want faster confidential dispute handling, arbitration could also be considered, but that depends on the commercial strategy.

\# 3\) SLA — detailed review

The SLA is helpful, but it needs tightening.

\---

\#\# A. Uptime commitments are good, but the table is unclear  
The uptime table appears to have a formatting issue:

\- “99.0% 99.5% (Professional / Navigator) | 10%”  
\- “95.0% 99.0% | 25%”

That looks like it may have been intended as:  
\- 99.0% to 99.5% \= 10%  
\- 95.0% to 99.0% \= 25%  
\- below 95.0% \= 50%

\#\#\# What to do  
Rewrite the service credit table in a cleaner format.

\---

\#\# B. Service credits need a clearer claim process  
The SLA says users request credits by email and Zawda responds in 10 business days.

That is good, but you should also add:  
\- what evidence must be attached  
\- who is eligible  
\- whether credits are automatically excluded if account is overdue  
\- whether credits expire  
\- whether credits are the exclusive remedy

You already say credits are the exclusive remedy. Good.

\#\#\# Stronger version  
Add:  
\> Requests must include dates, affected services, screenshots or logs where available, and the user account ID.

\---

\#\# C. Support response times are reasonable, but business hours should be defined carefully  
You defined business hours as Sunday–Thursday, 09:00–18:00 GST. Good.

\#\#\# But I would also clarify:  
\- whether weekends are excluded entirely  
\- whether the clock pauses on UAE public holidays  
\- what “received” means for ticket timing  
\- whether “response” means acknowledgement or substantive response

\#\#\# Why this matters  
Clients often confuse “we received your message” with “we solved the issue.”

\---

\#\# D. Advisory services section is useful, but should be even more explicit  
This is important for your business model.

The SLA says Navigator and Enterprise get advisory access, but excludes:  
\- legal advice  
\- direct regulator communication  
\- drafting legal documents  
\- representation in enforcement  
\- tax advice  
\- guaranteed outcomes

That’s good.

\#\#\# Recommended improvement  
Add a sentence like:

\> Advisory services are limited to operational guidance, regulatory interpretation at a general level, document review feedback, and process recommendations. They do not include legal review, formal sign-off, or professional certification.

This makes it much clearer for CFOs and compliance teams.

\---

\#\# E. Security commitments are solid, but should be supported by a privacy/security policy  
The SLA mentions:  
\- TLS 1.2+  
\- AES-256  
\- end-to-end encryption for Enterprise  
\- backups  
\- breach notifications

That is strong.

\#\#\# What I’d add  
For a compliance product, users will likely ask for:  
\- access controls  
\- MFA  
\- audit logs  
\- data segregation  
\- incident cooperation  
\- subprocessors list  
\- security testing frequency

Those can go in a separate security annex or privacy policy.

\#\# F. Alert delivery commitments are useful, but need a disclaimer  
The timing targets are good, but you should say:  
\- delivery times depend on source publication  
\- regulator websites may be delayed or unavailable  
\- translations may affect timing  
\- alerts are not official notices

You already say some of this. I’d just make it a bit more prominent.
